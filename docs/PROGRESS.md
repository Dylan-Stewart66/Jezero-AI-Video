**The station (round 3, 2026-10-08; awaiting the user's look)**
The user: the isometric station is the film's strongest idea; keep Infrastructure and Compliance as they are, rebuild Governance, Visibility and Cost to show what the real platform does, and plan them as one station so the pull-back reads as designed, not accreted. Each section's additions have their own colour.
- **Colours** (`COL.gov`, `COL.comp`, `COL.vis`, `COL.cost`, each `[dark, dim, top, edge, light]`): every step at the same OKLCH lightness and chroma as the infrastructure teal (L 0.37, 0.47, 0.57, 0.68, 0.85; C about 0.07), only the hue changing: teal 192 (kept), violet 304 (top `#816d9a`), blue 248 (`#557ba0`), amber 72 (`#927147`, warmed from 82, which read olive), muted rose 358 (`#9a6677`). All at least 0.18 OKLab from coral and 0.33 from mint; violet and blue nudged apart (the closest pair). Faces take the kit's tone ratios, edges the edge step.
- **Layout** (`35a-station.js`, `STATION.L`): requests enter at the front right (sources, gateway on the platform's front edge with a holding bay beside each lane and the log rail along its outer face), cross the platform in the three lanes (one beside each team's trays), and leave at the back left: each team's cost meter stands in its own lane where it leaves the platform, then a collector along the back-left edge carries all approved requests into the model hub beyond the back lane. Monitoring plinth behind the far edge (back right); evidence stack at the back corner (unchanged). The front lane's far end sits on the text column's edge, which is why nothing stands beyond it and the collector exists.
- **Traffic** (`STATION.reqs`): one cube per request on a fixed schedule; lanes phased so the three streams interleave on the collector. About one in six or seven requests rejected across all three lanes (shot 7: 3 of 20; whole film 11 of 83), two kinds: identity, stopped at the entrance strip; model not approved, stopped at the exit strip (the strip flashes coral; the unapproved model block's edges flash coral). Rejected cubes turn coral, move aside into the bay, hold 4s, shrink away. Approved cubes turn mint at the exit check and go to model A, B or C. Every request that reaches the gateway leaves a mark on the log rail (light violet; coral if stopped): a barcode that grows, deliberately unlike the evidence stack.
- **Models:** four two-tier blocks; A, B, C light their core as a request arrives; D (not approved) is dim with no spur.
- **Visibility:** four screens of different sizes on one plinth, dark faces, single-weight lines and flat fills (`KIT.flats`), no text: requests per account and model as a 3 by 4 grid (a cell lights as a request from that team reaches that model; the unapproved column stays dark), tokens per team as bars (a team's bar steps up the moment its request passes the gateway), response time under a dashed target, errors flat near zero.
- **Cost:** a meter per team (base, four posts, top plate, segments of 0.16, a solid stop bar at the budget: 11, 12 and 6 segments). The spend so far drops in, then each passing request adds one segment that slides in from the cube. The front team reaches its limit about 3.7s into the shot: its stop bar turns coral and its requests queue in front of the meter (`fate: 'queue'`); the others stay at least two segments below theirs while the station is in view. Label "Budget" on open ground below that meter.
- **Framings** are fitted, not hand-set: `isoFit(points, box)` (`20-kit.js`) solves the isometric framing that fits the given site points into a stage box. `GATE_SHOT` leaves room right of the sources for the labels; `VIS_SHOT` and `COST_SHOT` fit the whole station into the visual zone. **Planet, markers and crater floor (2026-10-08, the user's feedback, two rounds; awaiting their look)**
- **Jezero is one crater everywhere.** Rim radius 4.7 degrees (8% of the planet's diameter). Drawn analytically in the planet pass, never baked, so it is sharp from orbit to the floor; the isometric floor disc shades itself with the same code (`jzGround` in `MARS_SHADE_GLSL`). Round 1 replaced the black hole with a rust bowl; round 2 ("too light, especially in the descent") made the floor a third darker (`JZ_DARK` 0.4, linear) but still rust, the walls steeper and terraced (three wandering terraces, only drawn once the camera is close enough to see them, `jzTerr`, so they never read as rings from orbit), and added the rim's cast shadow across the far inner wall and the floor below it (`jzRimShadow`, sun only). Also: a raised lit rim, faint lighter ejecta in radial streaks, and a small fan-shaped delta on the western inner wall.
- **The floor is real terrain** (`jzDetail`), the same in the descent and the isometric shots: fine regolith grain, small dunes in patches, a few small craters at two sizes, sparse rocks with short shadows, and the levelled pad the grid sits on, cut a little into the surface (`MARS.U.uPad`, the grid plus 0.7 each side). The ground darkens slightly round the platform once its trays are down (`uPadAO`). Everything finer than a pixel fades out (band-limited by the footprint), so detail appears as the camera descends and nothing shimmers from orbit. Contour lines are gone. Each rock and crater lies inside its own noise cell, so one cell is checked, not nine (that kept playback at 60fps).
- **Light hand-over.** The planet's sun and the kit's key light are about 83 degrees apart. In the last 0.6s of the dive (20.3 to 20.9s) the floor's relief hands over from the sun to the key light (`MARS.U.uKeyMix`), so rocks, dunes and craters agree with the kit's own shadows; it hands back early in the ascent (frameH 10.5 to 15), before the walls show. Kit contact shadows still come from the shadow catcher and fall on the terrain.
- **No exposure settle any more.** Round 1 darkened the ground after landing (`PLANET.floorExpo` 0.5) to keep the grey secondary text above 4.5:1; with the darker floor it isn't needed, so `floorExpo` is 1. The text and tracker pass the check over the terrain.
- **The floor disc follows the sphere** (a 96-ring disc lowered onto the planet's surface), so it lies exactly on the planet and the descent's crossfade has no parallax. The kit stays on the flat plane at y 0 (it floats at most about 0.1 units at the grid's far corner, invisible). The planet skips pixels the opaque disc hides (`uDiscA`, `uDiscR`).
- **The opening's markers now spread over the terminator into the sunlit crescent** (`AGENTS`: `n.dot(SUN) < 0.3`, was `< -0.07`, night side only), so some pins cast visible shadows in shots 1 and 2. The arcs in shot 11 land on each pin's dot (`sp.heights`), not at its foot.
- The descent's bottom scrim under the tracker runs from a time-based hook (shot 5 stops drawing at 20.5s and cut it short) and holds to 22.6s.

The blended masters (26 GB) were deleted once the user was happy with the MP4; a re-encode now means a re-render (about 2.5 hours).

## The export (`scripts/render.mjs`)

The user's spec (2026-10-08): one file, `jezero-ai-launch-film-4k.mp4`; 3840x2160 (device scale factor 2) at 60fps; motion blur from blended subframes that only affects moving things, with still frames exactly as sharp as the browser; H.264 yuv420p, CRF 12 or lower, preset slow or slower. Quality first; size and render time don't matter.
- **Rendering:** Chrome (Playwright, `channel: 'chrome'`) with a real `--force-device-scale-factor=2` window sized so the page is exactly 1920x1080 CSS px, at `?record&scale=2` (world backing store 3840x2160). Playwright's `deviceScaleFactor` emulation was rejected: CDP captures from it come back 1920x1080, and its 4K screenshots are 8x slower (equally sharp; text lands 1 device pixel lower).
- **Motion blur:** 8 subframes per frame (`--sub`) spread evenly over a 180 degree shutter (`--shutter`) centred on the frame time, averaged in linear light. A frame whose 8 captures are identical is written byte for byte; still pixels in a moving frame come back exactly (the blend tables are checked for that at start-up). Frame 0's shutter wraps to 79.996s, so the blur is continuous across the loop.
- **Two browser behaviours found and handled in the export only (the film's code is unchanged):** (1) `.tb .lt { will-change: transform }` makes Chrome keep a text line's raster from mid-animation (the "Why?" slam's 1.6x scale, the rise's sub-pixel offsets) and resample it once settled: played through, settled text came out softer than a direct render of the same moment (max differences 54 to 210 levels at every shot checked). `render.mjs` injects `.tb .lt{will-change:auto !important}`; with it, played-through frames match direct renders (identical or within 1 level; the end card's QR label and corner mark differ by sub-pixel glyph placement, equally crisp). The same softness exists in live browser playback; consider dropping the hint in `style.css` if the HTML is ever shown live. (2) The first capture after the page loads can come back before new text is painted, so `render.mjs` settles one capture before starting and takes every subframe until two captures agree.
- **Output:** phase 1 writes blended frames as PNGs to `render-frames/` (resumable, 3 browser lanes, `meta.json` guards against mixing builds); phase 2 encodes them with ffmpeg-static: `zscale` to BT.709 limited range with spline36 chroma downsampling, libx264 `-preset veryslow -crf 10`, High profile, level 5.2, tagged bt709, `+faststart`. Measured: CRF 10 is within about 0.3 to 0.6 dB of a lossless yuv420p encode and keeps faint stars as well as lossless (98.7% of peak); the remaining difference from the browser render is yuv420p's half-resolution colour on saturated edges (coral text), inherent to the format.
- `sharp` and `ffmpeg-static` are now direct dev dependencies (ffmpeg still isn't installed system-wide).
- A known film detail, not an export issue: in the first frames of the "Why?" slam (8.0 to 8.1s, while it fades in at up to 1.6x) the "?" is clipped by its line's mask. The same happens in the browser.

## Technical notes

- **Render order:** stars, planet, markers and crater floor are in three's opaque pass (`transparent: false`, ordered by renderOrder) so the kit draws over them. A full-screen object flagged transparent would paint over the kit; that bug once made the trays invisible.
- **Mars** (`10-planet.js`): the surface and a relief-normal map are baked once into two 2048 cube maps from 3D noise, then ray-traced as an exact sphere each frame. Crater profiles must stay continuous (a step showed as a hard line close up; the delta's first version had one at its apex). Jezero (4.7 degrees, at world +Y) is not baked: `jzGround` in `MARS_SHADE_GLSL` gives its albedo, relief normal, light and shadow per pixel, and is shared by the planet pass and the floor disc. Ground detail is evaluated in world x, z (Jezero at the origin). `marsAlbedo` and `marsLight` are the planet's shading. The planet's centre is (0, -400, 0). `?fulllit` lights the planet from the camera for review. `flat` is a reserved word in GLSL 3 (it broke the first build of `jzGround`).
- **The descent** (`34-s05-reveal.js`): `DESCENT(t)` and `zoomMix` give a log zoom into the crater centre while fov eases from 36 to 7. The crater floor disc (`KIT.floorU`, its opacity and radius shared with the planet as `uDiscA`, `uDiscR`) fades in by camera frameH (invisibly, as it matches the planet) and widens to radius 78 after landing; it narrows back to Jezero's floor (`KIT.FLOOR_R`, about 24.6) early in the ascent. The same hook sets the light hand-over, the exposure, the pad's darkening and the descent's bottom scrim.
- **After the descent** (`40-s11-production.js`): `ASCENT(t)` for shot 11, then `POST(t)` for shots 11 to 13: a monotone cubic through keyed (time, yaw, pitch, planet x, y, radius), passed to `frameBody`, blended over the last seconds into shot 1's `planetDrift` continued back past the loop point, so position and speed match at 80s = 0s.
- **Kit fade:** `KIT.setFade(parts, edges)`. All kit Lambert materials are flagged transparent from the start (three compiles opaque materials to ignore opacity); at opacity 1 they look identical. Edges must fade first: they keep their 1.5px width at any distance, so on a small platform hundreds overlap and even a low alpha piles up to solid.
- **Fine lines** (`06-lines.js`): `makeLines({ curves, segs, color, width, parent, occlude, fade })`: screen-width strokes along 3D polylines, drawn progressively by `prog[k]`, faded by `alpha[k]`; `occlude` hides them behind the planet (which writes no depth). Used for the charts, the budget outline, the arcs and the trajectories. Must be `DoubleSide` (clockwise quads).
- **Leaders** are cleared at the start of every frame (`04-labels.js`), so jumping in time never leaves a finished shot's leader on screen.
- **Isometric kit** (`20-kit.js`): Lambert plus per-face vertex colour factors give exact three-tone faces (`KIT_LIGHT`, `faceFactor`); edges are instanced screen-space lines; one shadow-casting light. The site is turned 90 degrees (`SITE.rotY`), so the iso yaw is 135 in the world. `isoShot(sitePoint, px, py, frameH)` frames a point; `KIT.parts(geo, edgeSegs, count)` makes instanced parts. Trays are `TRAYS` (shot 6); the station's layout and traffic are `STATION` (`35a-station.js`); the gateway, tracks and models are `GATE` (shot 7); the scanner and evidence stack `SCAN` (shot 8); the monitoring area `MONITOR` (shot 9); the meters `COST` (shot 10). `PLATFORM_SHOT` is the resting whole-platform framing (shots 6 and 8). `KIT.flats(n)` gives unlit chart marks that fade with the kit.
- **Labels** (`04-labels.js`): `diagramLabel(...)` gives a fixed slot, an SVG leader and a 6px dot. Labels are also text blocks, so they get every text rule. World objects for the clearance rule are pushed to `OBJECTS`.
- **The layout check** (`scripts/check-layout.mjs`) samples every 0.1s over `BUILT` (now the whole film, `[0, DURATION]`; a full run takes a few minutes). It exempts a word crossfading into its own tracker word (matching `data-group`). Clearance uses axis-aligned screen boxes, so push a long diagonal object to `OBJECTS` in pieces (the scanner goes in as eight), or one box will cover empty space and fail a label that is actually clear.
- `--from` and `--to` check part of the film quickly (e.g. `--from 33 --to 41.5` for shot 8).
- **Performance:** live playback holds 60fps in every shot on this Mac. Shadow map is 2048 live, 4096 at `?scale=2`.
- Review aids: `?noedges` (kit without edge lines), `?nokit`, `?fulllit`, `?nobump`. In check mode `window.__dbg` exposes the main objects for probing.
