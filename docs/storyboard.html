The brief says “white on the background with a quiet zone”. I’m reading that as a white tile with dark modules and a quiet zone, as drawn on the end card. Some Android cameras fail on white modules over a dark background.

How I’ll build the world

Restraint and one kit. No bloom, glow, grain, gradients or transparency anywhere in the world layer.

Mars

  • A real sphere, lit with v1’s maths. v1’s planet is a flat billboard, which can’t hold its shape when the camera flies into a crater. I’ll move its terminator, night-side lift and limb shading onto a true sphere unchanged.
  • Surface from 3D noise in the planet’s own space, so there are no texture seams at any zoom: rust and ochre highlands, darker basalt plains, a small pale polar cap, four or five large crater rims, and Jezero as a small sharp crater.
  • Jezero is carved into the mesh. The sphere is densest around it, with a flat floor, a wall and a low rim, so the kit sits on a true flat floor. Finer detail fades in as the camera descends, so the surface holds up through a 50× zoom.
  • Atmosphere: a 2 to 3px rim on the lit limb only, inside the disc edge. Nothing outside the planet.
  • Space: flat #05070d with about 2,000 sharp one- and two-pixel stars. No twinkle halos.
  • Gentle surface motion comes from the camera orbit, plus a faint dust drift on a seamless 60-second cycle so the loop matches.

The isometric kit

  • One unit, g = 0.125 world units. Every footprint and height is a whole number of g, and everything sits on a 10g pitch.
  • Six primitives, built once and drawn as instanced meshes: tray (8 × 8 × 3g, from v1’s trayGeometry), plate, slab, square column, floor track and cube.
  • One matte material. Each face takes its colour from its direction: top the lightest step, left the middle, right the darkest. Every object reads in the same three tones from one key light. No metalness, no emissive.
  • Grounding: one directional light casts soft, tight contact shadows onto the floor and into the trays.
  • Edges: 1.5px screen-space lines, depth-tested, one tone lighter than the top face, the same weight everywhere. They won’t shimmer when the camera slides.
  • The site: 48 trays in three team groups with lanes between, a gate at the front edge, the evidence stack at the far corner and the chart panel along the back. Every crater shot is a camera slide across this one layout.

Camera

  • v1’s system, kept as it was: orbitShot, toOrbit, mixOrbit, and a scene list where each shot starts from the last one’s final orbit. Field of view and framing ease on a log scale for the dolly-zoom.
  • Planet shots orbit at fov 36°. Crater shots are true isometric: pitch 35.26°, yaw 45°, fov 7°.
  • Loop join: one move runs from 58.5s through 60s to 1.5s, so position and speed match across the seam.

Rendering and checks

  • 4× MSAA. The world renders at 2× for the MP4. Linear colour with one sRGB conversion at the end and no tone-mapping curve, so the ramp colours land exactly. A very gentle vignette is the only post effect.
  • One offline HTML file: three.js, Geist and Geist Mono (woff2 from the geist package, as base64), the QR generator and both logos inlined. window.seek(t) and window.DURATION; every frame is a pure function of time.
  • check-layout.mjs seeks every 0.1s and fails on overlap or spacing under 24px, text outside the safe area, label rules, shared slots and contrast under 4.5:1 sampled from the canvas. It also writes review/contact-sheet.png.
  • render.mjs: 3 subframes per frame blended with ffmpeg tmix, H.264, yuv420p, 60fps.
